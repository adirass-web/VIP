## 0. Executive Summary

> **⚠ POSITIONING UPDATE (2026-06-13, founder-approved) — propagated from `Copy_System_Brief.md`:** The category term **"digital safety reset" is retired.** The category is now **"private-client cyber protection."** Lead one-liner: **"Take back control of your digital life before it becomes leverage."** Category line: **"Personal Cyber Shield is private-client cyber protection: one senior expert who discreetly finds and closes what could be used against you."** Wherever this document still reads "digital safety reset," read "cyber protection"; the rhetorical line "changing passwords is not a digital safety reset" becomes "…is not cyber protection." `Copy_System_Brief.md` is the operative source of truth.

Personal Cyber Shield is **private-client cyber protection** for people entering high-stakes personal disputes or facing post-incident uncertainty.

The business should not be positioned as generic cybersecurity, IT support, investigation, surveillance defense, SaaS, or broad household tech help. The winning wedge is narrower and more commercially powerful:

> People in adversarial personal situations may be exposed through their digital life, but they do not know how, where, or why.

The buyer does **not** usually understand the technical vector. They fear that someone knows, sees, tracks, infers, asserts, or can expose something that should have stayed private. The service sells expert diagnosis, guided closure, and practical control — not a DIY checklist.

## 3. Corrected Wedge

### Problem With the Earlier Wedge

Earlier wording overemphasized:

- forgotten access;
- old access;
- open sessions;
- shared accounts;
- family plans;
- specific app examples.

Those are useful internally and in lower-funnel explanations, but dangerous as public hero copy.

If prospects hear “forgotten old access,” they may think:

> “I can fix that myself.”

That is commercially wrong. The buyer usually does **not** understand the exposure path. They feel uncertain, threatened, embarrassed, accused, tracked, watched, or outmaneuvered.

### Master Wedge

> **In personal disputes, as in enterprise cybersecurity worldwide, serious exposure rarely requires sophistication. It often comes from access, settings, apps, accounts, devices, shared services, recovery paths, online traces, and digital habits that ordinary people cannot fully see or evaluate. When the relationship turns adversarial, that exposure can become leverage. Personal Cyber Shield finds what is exposed, explains what matters, and closes what can be closed — in person, with you present.**

### Short Wedge

> **They may not need to hack you. They may only need what your digital life already exposes.**

### Core Phrase

> **Ordinary access becomes leverage.**

### Important Copy Rule

Use “ordinary access becomes leverage” as a high-level insight.

Do **not** over-explain it above the fold as:

- WhatsApp Web sessions;
- Apple recovery contacts;
- family phone plans;
- shared cloud folders;
- car apps;
- specific device settings.

Those examples belong lower in the funnel as vignettes, FAQ items, “what happens during the visit,” or service specs.

## 5. Buyer Psychology

### Core Buyer Fear

The buyer usually does **not** think:

> “My old access vectors remain open.”

The buyer thinks:

> “They know something they should not know.”  
> “They may be reading, tracking, watching, or listening.”  
> “They may have abused my phone, accounts, apps, cloud, car, or devices.”  
> “They may find something I need kept private.”  
> “They may use my digital life against me.”  
> “I do not know what is exposed.”  
> “I cannot trust my own phone, accounts, devices, or apps right now.”

### Strategic Implication

Public copy should not primarily say:

> “You probably forgot to close old access.”

It should say:

> **If they know, claim, imply, or assert something they should not know, you need a private expert review of your digital exposure — not guessing, panic changes, or DIY fixes.**

### Why DIY Framing Is Dangerous

| Public Message | Likely Buyer Reaction | Commercial Problem |
|---|---|---|
| “Old WhatsApp Web sessions may still be open” | “I can check that myself.” | Makes offer feel simple |
| “Shared Apple accounts may expose you” | “I’ll change my Apple password.” | Encourages partial DIY |
| “Family plans can reveal activity” | “I’ll leave the family plan.” | May cause escalation or incomplete fix |
| “Forgotten access points are dangerous” | “I’ll revoke access.” | Understates hidden complexity |

### Better Public Framing

| Better Message | Why It Works |
|---|---|
| “You may not know what is exposed — or how.” | Preserves need for expert diagnosis |
| “Do not guess your way through a high-stakes dispute.” | Positions service as risk-reducing |
| “Changing passwords is not a digital safety reset.” | Blocks the false solution |
| “One visible problem rarely tells you the full picture.” | Supports paid assessment |
| “We look across the personal digital environment, not one app.” | Differentiates from self-help |
| “The question is not only who had access. It is what your digital life still reveals.” | Expands beyond old access |
