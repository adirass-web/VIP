# C0. Workstream Objective

The purpose of this workstream is to make a high-trust, premium, in-person, sensitive B2C service bookable.

The buyer is anxious, exposed, and possibly in a legal, family, business, or safety-sensitive situation. They may be afraid that someone knows, sees, tracks, infers, or asserts something that should have stayed private. The business must make them feel that Personal Cyber Shield is serious, discreet, competent, bounded, and safe enough to contact.

This workstream defines:

1. Trust assets required before launch.
2. Conversion blocks required on the website.
3. Privacy and process reassurance.
4. Founder credibility architecture.
5. Sample certificate / private record positioning.
6. “What happens during the visit” page requirements.
7. “What we never do” boundaries.
8. FAQ for anxious buyers.
9. Contact-path design.
10. Attorney-safe trust language.
11. Reviews and testimonials strategy.
12. Conversion KPIs.

This workstream must prevent:

- fear-pumping;
- technical overwhelm;
- fake certainty;
- excessive legal or forensic claims;
- confusing the service with IT support;
- confusing the service with investigation;
- making the buyer think “I can fix this myself”;
- conversion friction from WhatsApp/Signal-only contact;
- trust collapse from insufficient founder proof.

# C1. Trust Problem Definition

## Core buyer trust barrier

The buyer is not only deciding whether the service is useful.

They are deciding whether to let Dr. LT into the most private parts of their digital life.

They may worry:

- Will you see my messages, photos, documents, or private accounts?
- Do I need to give you passwords?
- Will anything be uploaded?
- Will there be a record somewhere?
- Can my spouse, partner, family member, lawyer, employer, or adversary find out?
- Will I lose access to something?
- Will changing things alert the other side?
- Is this legal?
- Is this safe?
- Is this actually for me?
- Is this just expensive IT support?
- Is this a panic service exploiting my fear?
- Is the certificate a real legal artifact or just marketing?

The trust system must answer these questions before the client contacts.

# C4. Page / Asset 1 — What Happens During the Visit

## Objective

Make the in-person service feel calm, bounded, professional, and safe.

This page is critical because the client is being asked to invite a specialist into their home, office, or private location and review sensitive devices/accounts.

## Page title

**What Happens During the Visit**

Alternative:

**How a Private Digital Safety Reset Works**

## Required positioning

This page should explain the service without turning it into a technical checklist.

It should say:

> You stay present. Nothing is done behind your back. We work through the relevant devices, accounts, apps, and exposure points together, explain what matters, and document what was checked and changed.

## Required sections

### 1. Before the visit

Explain:

- client contacts via WhatsApp or Signal;
- minimal information at first contact;
- appointment is scheduled;
- client is told what to prepare;
- no forms or third-party CRM;
- if urgent, emergency surcharge applies;
- if safety-sensitive, timing and sequencing may change.

### 2. At the beginning

Explain:

- review the situation;
- identify what the client fears may be exposed;
- map the relevant devices, accounts, apps, services, and trails;
- set boundaries for the session;
- confirm what will and will not be done.

### 3. During the work

Explain:

- client remains present;
- changes are explained in plain English;
- no remote access;
- no cloud upload;
- no retained logs;
- identified exposure points are reduced where appropriate;
- if something exceeds scope, it becomes an add-on or later step.

### 4. At the end

Explain:

- client receives a plain-English summary;
- full-package clients receive a private dated record;
- attorney-friendly summary may be provided where appropriate;
- client receives practical next steps;
- Annual Shield Review may be offered after a full package.

### 5. What happens if something sensitive appears

Explain:

- no panic;
- no legal advice;
- no investigation of another person;
- no forensic reconstruction;
- may recommend attorney/safety/law-enforcement/specialist pathway where appropriate;
- work can pause if safety or legal risk appears.

## Conversion goal

After reading this page, a buyer should think:

> “I understand the process. It does not sound invasive, reckless, or mysterious.”

## Required CTA

> **Start with a Private Exposure Assessment — ₪3,500**

# C5. Page / Asset 2 — What We Never Do

## Objective

Create trust through explicit boundaries.

This service must not look like investigation, hacking, surveillance, legal evidence collection, or general tech support.

## Page title

**What We Never Do**

Alternative:

**Clear Boundaries for a Private Service**

## Required public copy

> Personal Cyber Shield is designed to reduce your own digital exposure. It is not a service for investigating another person, breaking into accounts, collecting legal evidence, or proving what happened in the past.

## Required boundary list

| Boundary | Public Explanation |
|---|---|
| **No remote access** | We do not take remote control of your devices. |
| **No cloud processing** | Your private data is not uploaded into a third-party system for analysis. |
| **No retained logs** | We do not keep a log of your digital life. |
| **No investigation of the other side** | The service is about your exposure, not their behavior. |
| **No offensive operations** | We do not access accounts, devices, or systems you do not control. |
| **No forensic reconstruction** | We do not claim to prove what happened in the past. |
| **No legal advice** | We do not replace your attorney. |
| **No guarantee of safety** | We reduce identified exposure; we do not promise no exposure remains. |
| **No routine IT support** | This is not computer repair or general tech troubleshooting. |
| **No panic changes in safety-sensitive cases** | In controlling-relationship situations, changes must be timed safely. |

## Required nuance

This page should not feel defensive. It should feel premium and disciplined.

Tone:

> Serious. Calm. Boundaried. Professional.

Avoid:

- legalistic intimidation;
- paranoid copy;
- technical over-explaining;
- long disclaimers above the fold.

# C6. Page / Asset 3 — Plain-English Privacy Promise

## Objective

Turn the operational trust model into consumer-facing value.

The TODO file specifically identified no remote access, no cloud processing, and no log retention as a major conversion differentiator if explained in plain outcome language.

## Core promise

> **In person. With you present. No remote access. No cloud processing. No retained logs.**

## Expanded version

> Your private digital life should not become another database. Personal Cyber Shield is delivered in person, with you present. We do not take remote control of your devices, upload your private data to a cloud system, or keep logs of your accounts, messages, files, or digital life.

## What this promise should communicate

| Operational Rule | Consumer Meaning |
|---|---|
| No remote access | Nobody works on your device invisibly |
| Client present | You see what is happening |
| No cloud processing | Your private data is not uploaded elsewhere |
| No retained logs | Your digital life does not become a file in a system |

The privacy promise must not imply that nothing is documented. The private dated record is a deliverable.

**Why Dr. LT**

Alternative:

**Why Personal Cyber Shield**

## Required positioning

> The same judgment used to understand serious cybersecurity exposure is applied here to a private consumer problem: when a personal dispute turns adversarial, the most damaging exposure may not look like a hack.

Explain:

- the client does not know what is exposed;
- visible symptoms are often incomplete;
- DIY fixes are often partial or mistimed;
- high-stakes disputes require judgment, not generic advice.

### 2. Why expert judgment matters

Explain:

- ordinary people cannot see the whole digital environment;
- the relevant exposure may be across devices, accounts, apps, metadata, online trails, recovery settings, shared services, or habits;
- the service interprets risk in context.

### 3. Dr. LT credibility

Include:

- professional authority;
- cybersecurity expertise;
- relevant public/professional roles;
- publications/speaking/advisory work if available;
- calm/private service posture;
- no sensational claims.

### 4. Why this is not IT support

Explain:

- IT support fixes devices;
- Personal Cyber Shield reviews exposure in an adversarial or post-incident personal context.

### 5. Why this is not investigation

Explain:

- the service protects the client’s own digital environment;
- it does not investigate the other side.

### 6. Why in-person

Explain:

- discretion;
- client presence;

# C10. Page / Asset 7 — Safe Contact Protocol

## Objective

Make WhatsApp/Signal-only contact feel intentional, safe, and easy.

The current model has no forms, no CRM, and direct messaging only. That is a trust asset, but only if explained.

> “I would like to book a private exposure assessment. The situation is [divorce / business dispute / inheritance / post-incident / other]. Please tell me the next step.”

Tell buyers not to send:

- passwords;
- private messages;
- screenshots;
- explicit accusations;
- legal documents;
- intimate images;
- account recovery codes;
- detailed allegations;

1. LT confirms whether the situation may fit.

3. Appointment is scheduled.
4. Client receives preparation instructions.
5. Sensitive details are discussed in the appropriate setting, not dumped into chat.

# C11. Attorney-Safe Trust Language

## Objective

Support attorney referrals without turning the service into legal evidence collection or forensic reconstruction.

## Attorney-facing promise

> Personal Cyber Shield helps clients in high-stakes personal disputes reduce their own digital exposure. The service is private, in-person, client-present, and focused on practical exposure reduction. It does not investigate the other side, reconstruct past events, or provide legal conclusions.

## Attorney-facing deliverable language

> Where appropriate, the client may receive an attorney-friendly summary describing the scope of work performed in plain English. This is not a forensic report, legal opinion, evidence collection document, or guarantee that no exposure remains.

## Attorney referral triggers

Attorneys may refer when a client says:

- “My spouse seems to know things they should not know.”
- “My ex may still have access to my phone/accounts.”
- “My business partner may have access to personal or business accounts.”
- “I think someone is tracking me.”
- “I received suspicious account alerts.”
- “I am afraid to change things because it may escalate.”
- “I do not know what my digital exposure is.”

## What attorneys need to know

| Attorney Question | Required Answer |
|---|---|
| Is this forensic? | No |
| Is this legal evidence collection? | No |
| Does it investigate the other side? | No |
| Does it preserve chain of custody? | No |
| Does it reduce client exposure? | Yes |
| Does the client receive a private dated record? | Full-package clients do |
| Is it safe for controlling relationships? | Only with safety-aware timing |
| Can the service coordinate with counsel? | Yes, with client permission |

# C12. Reviews and Testimonials Strategy

## Objective

Build B2C conversion proof without compromising client privacy.

Premium B2C conversion without reviews is difficult, but this service cannot use ordinary client stories carelessly.

## Allowed proof types

| Proof Type | Use |
|---|---|
| Anonymous testimonial | Best default |
| Sanitized case vignette | Strong if carefully anonymized |
| Attorney testimonial | Very valuable if available |
| Professional endorsement | Good for early trust |
| Private reference | Only by arrangement and with permission |
| Aggregate proof | “Common situations we handle” without client details |

## Review constraints

Do not ask clients to reveal:

- dispute type;
- personal facts;
- legal situation;
- names;
- family details;
- account details;
- adversary identity;
- location;
- specific exposure found.

## Sample testimonial prompt

> “You do not need to describe your situation. If you are comfortable, please write one or two sentences about whether the service helped you feel clearer, more in control, and better prepared.”

## Example safe testimonial format

> “The service was calm, discreet, and practical. I left understanding what had been reviewed and what had changed.”

## Post-service review workflow

1. Ask privately after successful engagement.
2. Offer anonymous format.
3. Provide safety-preserving prompt.
4. Get explicit approval before publication.
5. Store approved text only.
6. Do not retain underlying sensitive context.

## KPI

| KPI | Target |
|---|---:|
| Testimonial request rate | 100% of appropriate clients |
| Testimonial acquisition | 1–3 early proof points |
| Anonymous testimonial acceptance | Track |
| Client privacy complaints | Zero |
