## 5. Main sections

### 5.1 The problem isn't technical — it's context, privacy, and judgment
IT support fixes devices. The problem here is different: in a dispute, the question is what your digital life reveals, who can use it, and what is safe to change and when. That takes judgment under pressure, discretion, and an understanding of the whole picture — not a fix for one device.

### 5.2 Why your tech-savvy nephew is not the right call
A relative may know technology, but that isn't the issue. Informal help can miss the wider picture, make a partial or mistimed change, or — in a serious dispute — learn private things you'd rather they didn't, turning a sensitive matter into family knowledge. In safety-sensitive cases, the wrong change at the wrong time can escalate risk.

### 5.3 How this compares

| Alternative | Why it may seem enough | Why it isn't the same |
|---|---|---|
| Tech-savvy relative | Trusted, free, fast | Not private enough; may miss exposure context; creates family knowledge |
| IT support | Good at fixing devices | Not designed for adversarial personal exposure |
| Apple / Google / Microsoft support | Familiar vendor | Product support, not your dispute context |
| Password manager | Useful tool | Tool setup is not exposure diagnosis |
| Antivirus | Familiar category | Doesn't review your personal digital environment |
| Private investigator | Related dispute context | Investigates others; this reduces your own exposure |
| A DIY checklist | Cheap | Partial, mistimed, and easy to misread |

### 5.4 Why the first step is an assessment
Because the right move depends on your situation, the first step is a private, in-person Private Exposure Assessment — a senior expert reads your whole picture and tells you what matters and what to do next, before anything is changed in a hurry.
