## 5. Main sections

### 5.1 When to refer
Consider a referral when a client says:
- The other side seems to know things they shouldn't.
- They think their phone, accounts, or apps may be exposed.
- They changed passwords but still feel exposed.
- They're entering a divorce, separation, business dispute, or inheritance conflict.
- They fear that changing access could escalate danger.

### 5.2 What the service does
A senior cybersecurity expert reviews the client's own accounts, devices, apps, and access points in person, explains what matters in plain English, and reduces exposure where appropriate. The client stays present throughout.

### 5.3 What the service does not do
It does not investigate the other side, reconstruct past events, preserve chain of custody, collect evidence, or provide legal conclusions. It is not IT support and not a guarantee that no exposure remains.

### 5.4 How the assessment works
Most clients begin with a Private Exposure Assessment — a paid, in-person diagnostic (₪3,500, credited toward a full package within 14 days). If a full reset is warranted, it proceeds to Personal Shield or, for complex cases, Bespoke Private Protection.

### 5.5 What the client receives
A plain-English summary of the work performed. For full-package clients, a private dated record and — only with the client's explicit authorization — an attorney-friendly summary describing the scope of work performed. It is not a forensic report, legal opinion, evidence document, or certification.

### 5.6 Safety-sensitive cases
In controlling-relationship situations, timing matters. The work follows the client's safety plan and your guidance; changes that could escalate danger are planned, not rushed.

### 5.7 Referral protocol
Initial contact is by WhatsApp or Signal, by the client. We coordinate with you only with the client's permission. We ask clients not to send sensitive documents or detailed allegations in a first message.
