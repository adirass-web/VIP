## Access shared in good faith can still matter

Help with an account or a shared family service can connect several parts of your digital life. The effects may extend beyond the arrangement you remember making.

Dr. Tabansky examines the relevant connections within your authorized environment and agreed scope, including access to private data and the arrangements you depend on to manage your accounts. You gain an explanation of what remains connected and what separating it would mean.

Remaining access does not establish that a relative has used it or acted improperly.

## Understand the connections before changing them

Changing one password does not tell you what a shared service or recovery setting allows. Ending an arrangement can also affect access you rely on.

We explain the relevant connections and the effects of proposed changes. You decide what to authorize. The work concerns access you are authorized to manage; it does not decide ownership or legal entitlement to family or estate information.

## Your information and your attorney

You remain present, approve material changes and control your credentials. Toza does not keep them for ongoing use, remotely control your devices, monitor them or install security software on them.

A family connection does not authorize work on someone else's account or device. Toza does not determine who is entitled to an account, document or asset. We do not investigate relatives, reconstruct past events forensically, collect evidence or give legal advice.

With your authorization, an attorney-friendly summary can describe the work and remaining issues. A referral does not automatically give counsel access to findings or personal information. The summary is not a forensic report or evidence document, and the work does not guarantee that every exposure is found or removed.
